The night is not only a till. A room has a mood, music has to fit the crowd, drunk guests start fights, and inspectors check the paperwork. **Why:** these systems turn running a club into a set of trade-offs (spend on a DJ, on security, on permits) instead of a pricing puzzle.

All random draws come from the seeded deterministic generator described in [The night loop](/engine/night-loop), so the same seed replays the same fights and the same inspector.

## The 0–100 stats

Reputation, hype, safety, guest satisfaction and the crowd-energy target are all bounded with

$$
\operatorname{clamp}(v) = \min\big(100,\; \max(0,\; v)\big)
$$

| Stat | Starts at | Moved by | Bound |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reputation $R$ | 20 | night result, fights, shutdown, promo backfire | clamp at every change; $R \le 0$ is a defeat |
| Hype $H$ | 0 | promos, DJ and band bookings, daily decay | clamp; decay floors at 0 |
| Safety | 100 | fights, daily recovery | clamp; recovery caps at 100 |
| Satisfaction (per guest) | 60 | every minute, see below | clamp at the end of each guest's minute |
| Energy target $E^\ast$ | n/a | room, music, crowd moves | clamp |
| Violations | 0 | failed inspections, forcing the door | not clamped; 3 is a defeat |

## Hype

Hype is a day-level stat: it fills the club tonight (it is the largest term in the head-count formula on [Guests & spending](/engine/guests)) and fades by 10 every day.

$$
H \leftarrow \operatorname{clamp}(H + \Delta H), \qquad
\Delta H = \begin{cases}
h_c & \text{promo on channel } c \\
\operatorname{round}(0.3 \cdot \text{fanbase}) & \text{booking a DJ or a band} \\
30 & \text{controversial promo}
\end{cases}
$$

where $h_c$ is 4, 6, 10, 14 or 25 for Posters, X, Instagram, TikTok and Influencer and 0.3 is the DJ hype factor. Each promo costs one CEO energy point and can run once per day.

The **controversial promo** buys +30 hype for 3000 coin, but backfires with probability 0.25: reputation $-20$ and an inspector is guaranteed tonight. It is refused while reputation is 20 or less, so a backfire alone can never end the game.

## Crowd energy

Crowd energy $E_t \in [0, 100]$ is the room's mood during the night. **Why:** a half-empty room with nobody dancing feels dead, and the player should feel the difference between a packed floor and a quiet bar.

Energy starts at 30 and moves 10% of the way toward a target each minute:

$$
E_{t+1} = E_t + 0.1\,\big(E^\ast_t - E_t\big)
$$

Since $E_0 = 30$ and $E^\ast_t \in [0, 100]$, each step is a convex combination, so $E_t$ stays in $[0, 100]$ without a clamp. The target is

$$
E^\ast_t = \operatorname{clamp}\Big( 100\,\big(0.55\, D_t + 0.45\, \Phi_t\big) + 5\,(u_{\text{snd}} + u_{\text{lgt}}) + 15\, \beta^{\text{band}}_t + X_t \Big)
$$

$$
\Phi_t = \min\!\Big(1,\; \frac{n_{\text{in}} + 6\, n_{\text{host}}}{0.95\, C}\Big), \qquad
D_t = \min\!\Big(1,\; \frac{n_{\text{dance}} / n_{\text{in}}}{0.85}\Big)
$$

where

- $\Phi_t$ is how full the room looks: guests inside $n_{\text{in}}$, each host counting as 6 guests, against 95% of capacity $C$.
- $D_t$ is the dancing share, maxing out when 85% of guests dance ($D_t = 0$ in an empty room). Guests' states are those chosen in the previous minute.
- $u_{\text{snd}}, u_{\text{lgt}} \in \lbrace 0, 1, 2, 3 \rbrace$ are the sound and lighting upgrade tiers (+5 energy each).
- $\beta^{\text{band}}_t$ is the band's multiplier while a band plays, else 0.
- $X_t$ is the sum of active crowd moves (below).

**Crowd moves** are player actions with a duration and a cooldown. Each repeat is weaker: power $= \max(0.2,\; 1 - \text{fatigue} \cdot \text{uses})$. The DJ anthem instead uses power 1.2 if the DJ matched the crowd, 0.7 if not.

| Move | Energy added to $E^\ast$ | Duration / cooldown (min) | Fatigue | Needs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MC hype | $25 \cdot$ power (and +0.15 · power satisfaction per minute) | 20 / 60 | 0.3 | an MC |
| Hosts to the floor | $\min(40, 6\, n_{\text{host}}) \cdot \text{power} \cdot (1 - E_t/200)$ | 30 / 90 | 0.2 | a host |
| DJ anthem | $35 \cdot$ power (and +10 · power satisfaction once, stay +45 · power min) | 20 / 360 | 1 | a DJ on the decks |
| Sparkler parade | $15 \cdot$ power (and bottle sales to copycats) | 10 / 45 | 0.35 | booked tables, after minute 90 |

### What energy does

From Level 3 (the first venue with a dance floor), energy shapes every guest's minute:

| Effect | Formula |
| --- | --- |
| Mood drift per minute | $0.05\,\frac{E_t - 50}{50} + 0.01\, u_{\text{lgt}}$ |
| Dance chance multiplier | $\operatorname{lerp}(0.6, 1.4;\, E_t / 100)$ |
| Bar chance multiplier | $\operatorname{lerp}(0.8, 1.25;\, E_t / 100)$ |
| Early exit | if $E_t \lt 35$ and satisfaction $\lt 45$: leaves with probability 0.02 per minute |
| Encore | at their leave minute, if $E_t \ge 75$: stays 25 more minutes with probability 0.5 (once, not past closing) |

where $\operatorname{lerp}(a, b; x) = a + (b - a)\operatorname{clamp}_{[0,1]}(x)$.

### Guest satisfaction per minute

Putting the pieces together, a guest inside who does not leave this minute gets

$$
\text{sat}_{t+1} = \operatorname{clamp}\Big( \text{sat}_t + \underbrace{s^{\text{room}}_t + 0.05\,\kappa_t + 0.15\,\text{pow}_{\text{mc}}\,\mathbb{1}_{\text{mc}} + 0.01\, n_{\text{host}}\,\mathbb{1}_{\text{man}}}_{\text{ambient}} + 0.6\,\text{pnl}_t + a_t \Big)
$$

where $s^{\text{room}}_t$ is the energy drift above (0 below Level 3), $\kappa_t = \min(1, 60\, n_{\text{rel}} / n_{\text{in}})$ the Guest Relations coverage, $\text{pnl}_t$ the guest's paper profit on tokens, and $a_t$ the action term:

$$
a_t = \begin{cases}
+5 \;(+2 \text{ per round bought}) & \text{bought a token} \\
-10 & \text{token out of stock} \\
-1 & \text{waiting at a full bar} \\
0.15 \cdot \max\big(m^{\text{DJ}}_t,\, m^{\text{band}}_t\big) \cdot (1 + 0.1\, u_{\text{snd}}) & \text{danced} \\
-0.05 & \text{idle}
\end{cases}
$$

At the end of the night, the average of this number over every admitted guest decides reputation (see [Reputation](#reputation-from-the-night)).

## The DJ (rule R4)

A DJ booking (one per night) brings hype now and lifts the dance floor later. It is usually made by day, but a DJ can still be booked after the doors open, as long as it is before minute 60 (23:00), when the set starts. At minute 60 (23:00) the DJ takes the decks and reads the room:

$$
m^{\text{DJ}} = \begin{cases}
1 + 0.2 = 1.2 & g_{\text{DJ}} = \hat g \\
1 - 0.15 = 0.85 & g_{\text{DJ}} \ne \hat g
\end{cases}
\qquad
\hat g = \operatorname*{arg\,max}_{g} \#\lbrace \text{guests inside with genre } g \rbrace
$$

where $\hat g$ is the dominant genre (ties go to the genre seen first; if the club is empty it falls back to all of tonight's guests, then to the club's own genre). Before minute 60, and on nights without a DJ, $m^{\text{DJ}} = 0.5$: dancing to the house playlist is half as satisfying. The DJ's arrival also pumps the token market by 0.06 that minute.

**Matching the genre.** The player cannot see the room before 23:00, but can forecast it. Since each guest likes the club genre with probability 0.4 and otherwise a uniform genre of their segment, the expected genre share is

$$
\Pr(\text{genre} = g) = 0.4\,\mathbb{1}_{g = g_{\text{club}}} + \sum_{s} 0.6 \cdot \frac{w_s}{\sum_j w_j} \cdot \frac{\mathbb{1}_{g \in G_s}}{\lvert G_s \rvert}
$$

where $w_s$ are tonight's segment weights (promos included) and $G_s$ the segment's genres. Promoting to the right segment is how a player steers the room toward the DJ they booked.

## Live bands

A band is booked by day, one per night. Its set is fixed when the night opens, from whether a DJ is booked by then:

$$
\text{set} = \begin{cases}
[0, 60) & \text{a DJ is booked: opening act, 22:00–23:00} \\
[30, 180) & \text{no DJ: main act, 22:30–01:00}
\end{cases}
$$

A DJ booked later in the night doesn't move the band: a paid act always plays its slot, and the two simply overlap.

When the set starts, the band reads the room with the same R4 rule, giving $m^{\text{band}} \in \lbrace 1.2, 0.85 \rbrace$. While it plays, it adds $15\, m^{\text{band}}$ to the energy target and dancers use $\max(m^{\text{DJ}}, m^{\text{band}})$, so a band rescues the pre-DJ hour from the 0.5 playlist factor.

## Tilt and fights

Tilt $\tau \ge 0$ is how wound-up a guest is. It has no upper bound. Each minute:

$$
\tau \leftarrow \max(0,\; \tau - 0.1) \;+\; 8\,\max(0, -\text{pnl}) \;+\; \text{fomo}_k \cdot \mathbb{1}_{\text{bought } k}
$$

where $\text{fomo}_k$ is 0 to 22 depending on the token, added once per purchase (a round bought for someone else adds none). A guest with $\tau \ge 80$ is **at risk** and may start a fight (rule R9). Each minute, an at-risk guest fights with probability

$$
p_{\text{fight}} = \begin{cases}
0.003 & n_{\text{sec}} \cdot 40 \lt n_{\text{in}} \quad \text{(understaffed)} \\
0.0002 & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
$$

where $n_{\text{sec}}$ is the security staff on the books and $n_{\text{in}}$ the number inside at that moment. One guard per 40 guests cuts the fight rate fifteen-fold. A fight throws the guest out and costs

$$
\text{Safety} \leftarrow \operatorname{clamp}(\text{Safety} - 15), \qquad R \leftarrow \operatorname{clamp}(R - 5)
$$

### Security escorts

Guards act on their own before trouble starts. Each minute, before the guests act, with $\epsilon_t$ escorts still in progress:

$$
\text{free}_t = \max(0,\; n_{\text{sec}} - \epsilon_t)
$$

The $\text{free}_t$ most tilted at-risk guests are walked out, and each escorting guard is busy for 6 minutes. At-risk guests beyond that wait, and roll for fights. The player can also send security to a specific at-risk guest from an alert.

| Parameter | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Tilt decay | 0.1 / min |
| Fight threshold | 80 |
| Guests per guard | 40 |
| Fight chance (understaffed / staffed) | 0.003 / 0.0002 per min |
| Safety / reputation per fight | −15 / −5 |
| Escort time | 6 min |
| Security wage | 400 / day, from Level 3 |

## Permits, inspections and violations

A licensed club has to keep its permits current. **Why:** cutting corners on paperwork should be a gamble with real odds, not a free saving.

A permit $j$ is active on day $d$ if it is valid and $d \lt \text{expires}_j$. Renewing an active permit extends it from its current expiry, so renewing early loses nothing.

| Permit | Cost | Days | From level | Required at |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Business permit | 1000 | 30 | 1 | all levels |
| OTC trading licence | 5000 | 30 | 2 | Level 2 |
| Trading-floor licence | 15000 | 30 | 3 | Levels 3–5 |
| Crowd permit | 10000 | 7 | 4 | over 150 inside |

### The inspection roll (R6)

When the club opens, an inspector is scheduled with probability

$$
p_{\text{insp}} = \begin{cases}
1 & \text{forced (controversial-promo backfire or a scenario event)} \\
\max\big(p_0,\; 0.2\big) & \text{Safety} \lt 40 \\
p_0 & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
\qquad
p_0 = \begin{cases}
0.4 & \text{a required permit is missing or expired} \\
0.05 & \text{all in order}
\end{cases}
$$

at a uniform minute in $\lbrace 0, \dots, 359 \rbrace$. Fights get reported: a club with low safety draws inspectors even with perfect paperwork.

### The visit

The inspection **passes** if no required permit is missing and no underage guest was admitted tonight (rule R8: underage guests get in when there is no security from Level 3 on). Otherwise:

$$
\text{coin} \leftarrow \text{coin} - 5000, \qquad \text{violations} \leftarrow \text{violations} + 1
$$

and if the on-premise licence is missing while the takeaway one is active (rule R16), the venue is **sealed** for 1 night: the next night has no guests.

### Crowd permit (R7) and the door (R10)

The first minute more than 150 guests are inside, the engine rolls once: without an active crowd permit, the club is **shut down** with probability 0.5. Everyone leaves, and at the report

$$
\text{coin} \leftarrow \text{coin} - \operatorname{round}\big(0.5\,(\text{bar} + \text{cover} + \text{table})\big), \qquad R \leftarrow \operatorname{clamp}(R - 10)
$$

Forcing the door open past capacity (R10) is an immediate violation, once per night.

| Parameter | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Inspection chance, permits in order | 0.05 |
| Inspection chance, permit missing | 0.4 |
| Low-safety threshold / chance | 40 / 0.2 |
| Fine | 5000 |
| Crowd threshold / shutdown chance | 150 / 0.5 |
| Shutdown revenue / reputation penalty | 50% / −10 |
| Sealed nights (R16) | 1 |
| Violations to lose | 3 |

## Reputation from the night

At the report, the average satisfaction $\bar S = \operatorname{round}\big(\frac{1}{\lvert A \rvert}\sum_{g \in A} \text{sat}_g\big)$ over all admitted guests $A$ sets the reputation change (rule R11):

$$
\Delta R = \begin{cases}
\operatorname{round}\!\Big(3 + 5 \cdot \dfrac{\bar S - 70}{30}\Big) & \bar S \ge 70 \\
0 & 40 \le \bar S \lt 70 \\
-5 & \bar S \lt 40
\end{cases}
\qquad R \leftarrow \operatorname{clamp}(R + \Delta R)
$$

A good night is worth +3 to +8; a bad one costs 5. With no admitted guests, reputation does not change.

| Parameter | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Good night | 70 |
| Bad night | 40 |
| Gain range | 3–8 |
| Bad-night loss | 5 |

<Accordion title="Worked example: one minute of a busy Nightclub">

Level 4 (capacity 190), 150 guests inside, 60 of them dancing, 2 hosts, 3 guards, sound and lighting at tier 1, no band, no crowd move, energy $E_t = 40$.

**Energy.**

$$
\Phi = \min\!\Big(1, \tfrac{150 + 12}{0.95 \times 190}\Big) = 0.898, \quad
D = \min\!\Big(1, \tfrac{60/150}{0.85}\Big) = 0.471
$$

$$
E^\ast = 100\,(0.55 \times 0.471 + 0.45 \times 0.898) + 10 = 76.3, \qquad
E_{t+1} = 40 + 0.1\,(76.3 - 40) = 43.6
$$

The room is filling up, so energy climbs about 3.6 points this minute.

**Fights.** 3 guards cover $3 \times 40 = 120$ guests, fewer than 150, so the club is understaffed: each at-risk guest fights with probability 0.003 per minute, about $1 - 0.997^{60} = 16.5\%$ over an hour. A fourth guard ($160 \ge 150$) drops that to $1 - 0.9998^{60} = 1.2\%$.

**Reputation.** If the night ends with average satisfaction 82, $\Delta R = \operatorname{round}(3 + 5 \times 12/30) = 5$.
